## 1. ACTUAL SOURCE-SHAPED contour coordinate test: a physical curvature difference

A shortest path from lattice site \(y=(0,0)\) to \(x=(1,1)\) inside a legal L=13 block has precisely two coordinate-order permutations, \(\gamma_{xy}\) and \(\gamma_{yx}\). Such paths are part of CMP109's set G(y,x) in its (0.3)–(0.11). The older CMP98 construction may use a selected \(\Gamma_{y,x}\) path; this subsection compares **source contour coordinates**, NOT the complete coarse-link M operations.

For a Lie-algebra-valued small fine field \(U_b(t)=\exp(tA_b)\), differentiation of path ordered holonomy at t=0 is \(P_\gamma A=\sum_{b\in\gamma}\epsilon_b A_b\). By CMP109's symmetric group-average axiom (0.8),
\[
 F_{\rm new} A=P_{\rm sym}A=\tfrac12(P_{\gamma_{xy}}A+P_{\gamma_{yx}}A).
\]
Choose the CMP98-style selected source contour \(\Gamma_{y,x}=\gamma_{xy}\); then \(F_{\rm old}=P_{\gamma_{xy}}\). Order the four directed edges \((00\to10,10\to11,01\to11,00\to01)\), with A=(a1,a2,a3,a4). We have
\[
 F_{\rm old}A=a_1+a_2,\quad
 F_{\rm new}A=\tfrac12(a_1+a_2+a_3+a_4),\quad
 (dA)(p)=a_1+a_2-a_3-a_4.
\]
Thus
\[
 \boxed{(F_{\rm new}-F_{\rm old})A=-\tfrac12(dA)(p).}\tag{R118.1}
\]
This is a true first-order source contour-coordinate result, contingent only on the stated selected-old-path versus symmetric-new-two-path choice; it makes **no claim** that full CMP98 (15), CMP99 Q_j or CMP109 (0.4) coarse averages differ by exactly this amount.

On a pure gradient \(A=d\lambda\), \(d^2\lambda=0\) and \(F_{\rm new}d\lambda=F_{\rm old}d\lambda=\lambda(x)-\lambda(y)\). But the field A=(0,1,0,0) gives curvature 1 and \(F_{\rm new}A-F_{\rm old}A=-1/2\). So the contour-coordinate change is **not identically zero on physical curvature**.

In the unweighted four-edge source-local test the one-row Euclidean operator norms are \(\|F_{\rm old}\|=\sqrt2\), \(\|F_{\rm new}\|=1\), \(\|F_{\rm new}-F_{\rm old}\|=1\) per Lie-color component. These are NOT a global bound on the whole gauge-fixing operator F; different rows overlap paths.

## 2. Source-corrected distinction: a curvature-sensitive GAUGE COORDINATE is not automatically a physical change of GAUGE ORBIT

Both path-coordinate systems have the same differential along a based-gauge gradient:
\[
 F_{\rm old}K=F_{\rm new}K=I
\]
on a full collection of holonomies from each block's coarse center to all independent fine vertices, in the real identity-background chart with compatible endpoints. Here K is the based-site infinitesimal gauge gradient (orientation convention fixed).

Let \(R_{\rm curv}=F_{\rm new}-F_{\rm old}\). Then \(R_{\rm curv}K=0\), and the source-motivated axial/holonomy gauge-slice projections obey
\[
 \boxed{(I-KF_{\rm new})-(I-KF_{\rm old})=-K R_{\rm curv}.}\tag{R118.2}
\]
The difference between the gauge-fixed REPRESENTATIVES is a **pure fine gauge gradient**, albeit with a parameter determined by curvature. Therefore the R118.1 curvature dependence of a *gauge-fixing coordinate* is **not evidence of a gauge-invariant observable mismatch**. The physical Wilson action on the same gauge orbit is invariant; the explicit gauge section and its F norm change.

This sharpens R114–R117: changing gauge coordinates can be a curvature-dependent **slice shear** while leaving the underlying orbit unchanged, provided F K=I and both are admissible on the same source chart. Crucially this does not prove equivalence of the full coarse-field averaging maps, which must be compared independently.

**Additional high-value warning:** at SU(2) identity the derivative of a **traced** Wilson loop at zero field vanishes for *all* Lie-algebra directions because \(\mathrm{tr}(X)=0\) on su(2). Thus one cannot use a nonzero first derivative of the Wilson trace to detect R118.1. First-order Lie-algebra plaquette flux is gauge-covariant, while a traced-loop discrimination requires second-order expansion or a nontrivial background.
