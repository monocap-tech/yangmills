# YM-R78 — additive terminology registry

Date: 2026-10-10
Prior live cursor: YM-R78 (from YM-R77 checkpoint).
Scope: newly defined load-bearing terms only. R01-R77 wording and terminology remain unchanged.

- GaugeFixedGaussianEffectiveAction: on a finite actual constrained carrier with H=[[A,B],[B*,C]], C>0, integration over eliminated Euclidean Gaussian coordinates gives (1/2)<r,(A-BC^-1 B*)r>+(1/2)log det C plus background-independent normalization, PROVIDED the chart measure is the specified Lebesgue Gaussian and Jacobians are separately accounted for. Abstract definition, not a CMP109 object identification.
- GaussianDeterminantJet: mixed second derivatives in source-defined external coordinates of (1/2)log det C. The external coordinates must not be confused with momentum p without a primary dictionary.
- NormalizedLogDetJet: GaussianDeterminantJet divided by m=dim(eliminated carrier), when the trace normalization is justified by the source action-density convention.
- DeterminantDimensionBudget: necessary volume/mode-count factor m when passing from normalized traces to the unnormalized determinant action or local F^2 coefficient.
- CompleteGaussianMismatch: the sum of reduced-Hessian and GaussianDeterminantJet differences on a common source-identified carrier, excluding all non-Gaussian interactions, measure Jacobians and localization.
- FullPolarizationNonGaussianDefect: difference of the remaining interaction/cumulant, gauge/measure and localization contributions to the full one-step polarization, not controlled by finite Schur algebra.
- PrimaryCMP109EquationWindow: original paper Eqs (5.37), (5.42), (0.29), including preceding definitions and succeeding quantifiers; not yet equation-readable in this environment.

Classification: ABSTRACT-DERIVED indicates rigorous finite-dimensional derivation with declared hypotheses; CONDITIONAL-NATIVE indicates an application requiring actual Yang–Mills source identification; OPEN-PRIMARY indicates a needed original equation is uninspected. None by themselves mean a mass-gap theorem or a continuum construction.
